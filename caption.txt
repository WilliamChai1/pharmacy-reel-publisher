As a community pharmacist here, one of the most common questions I get from worried parents and grandparents is: *"Aunty/Uncle Pharmacist, how many spoons of syrup should I give my child?"* 🥄❌

Here is a little secret that might surprise you: **Using normal kitchen spoons to measure medicine is actually quite dangerous.** A standard kitchen teaspoon can hold anywhere from 2.5ml to 7ml! That means you might be under-dosing your child (so they take longer to get well) or—worse—accidentally giving them an overdose. 

Watch our quick cartoon reel above to see why kitchen spoons are a big no-no, and how you can make medication time safe and stress-free! 👧🏻👦🏻💊

Here are 3 quick tips to keep in mind:
• **Ditch the cutlery:** Always use the oral syringe or measuring cup that comes inside the medicine box.
• **Dose by weight, not age:** A child's medicine dosage depends entirely on how heavy they are, not how old they are.
• **Ask your pharmacist:** When in doubt, always double-check with us before giving any new syrup!
